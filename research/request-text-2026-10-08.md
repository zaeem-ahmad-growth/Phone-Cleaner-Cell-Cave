# Request for specific guidance, sent to the Google Play review team — 8 Oct 2026

Sent after Google declined our appeal and quoted the **Misleading Claims** sub-clause of the
Deceptive Behavior policy without naming the failing element. Reproduced verbatim in tab 01,
under "Humble Request to Google Review Team".

The list of corrections is deliberately **not** repeated here — it is already in
[the appeal text](appeal-text-2026-10-08.md), and the site link lets the review team verify
every claim against the app screen that performs it.

**Revised 9 Oct 2026 — the support field holds 1,000 characters including spaces.** The body is
**998**. That ceiling, not taste, decides the wording: the four paragraphs of substance cost 888
on their own, so the opening thanks, the closing courtesy and the two asks are written to the
character.

What the letter must keep, and does:

- The plea, almost verbatim — new developer account, small team, *many months of our work*, not
  disputing the decision, asking for support, the risk to every other app on the account. This
  is the argument. A reviewer with discretion responds to it; the asks are only what they do
  after deciding to help. An earlier pass cut it to buy room and the letter went flat.
- An explicit **call to action**: *"Please do take a quick look at what we have put right: the
  link above opens it, and a few minutes of your time would show how seriously we take this."*
  It names the link, asks for it to be opened, and puts a time to it. The clause says what
  looking would **prove** — our seriousness — rather than the weaker "whether we have
  understood", which asked the reviewer to grade us instead of giving them a reason to click.
- **"A few minutes of your time", never a claim about the page.** A draft said "it is one page,
  and a few minutes"; the page prints to 17 pages. Asking for minutes of *their* time is a
  request. Asserting the page is short is a claim — and an unverifiable claim inside a letter
  answering a Misleading Claims finding repeats the offence. Never reintroduce one.
- **"Reinstated rather than republished", never "replaced".** Republishing is the actual
  alternative we face — the same app under a new package — and it is the word a developer would
  use. "Replaced" is vague about what would happen to the app.
- **No deadline we could be held to.** The letter promised to act "the same day"; if the reply
  asks for a change inside the app, a same-day promise becomes a claim we cannot keep, in a case
  that is already about claims. It now reads **"We will do whatever you ask, however much work
  it takes"** — unconditional commitment, no clock. The same wording closes the "What we are
  hoping for" panel. Never put a date or a turnaround in this correspondence.
- **"Functionally impossible"** — the operative phrase in the clause Google quoted — as the one
  way to ask about the **app** rather than the listing in their own words. A question, never a
  confession: name no feature of our own as suspect.

Spent to make the ceiling, in this order: the closing "Thank you for your time and support" (the
call to action carries the gratitude); "our whole portfolio and" (said again by "every other app
on this account"); the two ask paragraphs merged into one; the articles in the element list; and
"If the corrected listing now meets the policy" down to "If it meets the policy". **If room is
needed again, take it from the asks — never from the plea, the corrections line or the call to
action.**

Please do take a quick look at what we have put right: the link above opens

Cell Cave
