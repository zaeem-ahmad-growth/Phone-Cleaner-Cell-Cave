## Rules this message keeps

All the durable rules from `request-text-2026-10-08.md` still hold, and three are new:

- **The similarity is one short line, and nothing more.** The owner cut a detailed version that
  gave the dates and quoted the three differing words. Reciting what a reviewer wrote reads as
  an accusation of sending a template, and a reviewer who feels accused stops reading. The line
  says only that both replies carry the same text, and turns at once to the worry that matters
  — that our corrections were never seen. **Never put dates, quoted wording or greetings back
  into it.**
- **The link paragraph is two plain sentences and ends in the ask.** An earlier version described the page in one long clause and closed on a flourish - that looking would "show you more about us than anything we can write". A hurried reader skims an abstract sentence and a compliment. It now names the two things on the page in short clauses and ends with the request itself. **Keep it to two sentences; the last one is always the ask.**
- **The call to action sits in the first paragraph**, before the link, because the likeliest
  outcome is another automated reply that never reaches the second paragraph. "Before this case
  is closed" is the only acknowledgement that this may be the last exchange; no other pressure.
- **The account risk is argued in Google's own words.** Their notice says a suspension "counts
  as strikes against the good standing" of the account. Quoting their sentence back is stronger
  than describing our fear, and it carries the reason for reinstating rather than republishing:
  publishing again under a new package name leaves the strike standing — which is also the
  remedy their own step 3 proposes, answered without contradicting them.

Unchanged and not to be trimmed: the plea (new account, small team, many months of our work,
not disputing the decision, every other app on the account at risk); "a few minutes of **your**
time", never a claim about the evidence page; "reinstated rather than republished", never
"replaced"; no date or turnaround promise; and "functionally impossible" used as their operative
phrase in a question, naming no feature of ours as suspect.
